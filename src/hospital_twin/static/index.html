Inside the rooms

Not just deliveries. Every room has work a robot can take.

Tasks that are the same every time go to a robot. Tasks that change each time get a robot to assist while staff decide. Anything that needs judgment stays with people. Where no product exists yet, Amed marks it as a gap worth funding.

What you walk away with

A building plan and a business case, from one floor plan.

Facilities gets a list of what to change. Finance gets the hours saved and how many robots are worth buying. Nursing gets to see the shift before it happens.

  1. For facilities

    A door-by-door fix list

    Every doorway measured against the robot you picked, with the ones that are too narrow called out so you know exactly what to widen or re-hang.

  2. For the floor

    Where robots can go

    Each room marked as robot-ready, needs a hand-off point, or staff only, so you can plan drop-off spots before anything is installed.

  3. For finance

    Hours back, per shift

    Nurse walking time with and without robots on the same unit and the same orders, so the savings are about your building, not a brochure.

  4. For planning

    How many robots to buy

    The same shift run with one, two and three robots on good days and bad, showing the point where adding another stops paying off.
